### How the three parts interact — why they cannot be settled apart

The trigger sets how many movers *qualify*; the cap decides how many of those are paid for; the cadence multiplies whatever survives by 13; and the held book adds a fixed ~54% surcharge to every one of those 13 regardless of how many movers there were. So a day's paid intraday bill is roughly `13 × (movers-under-cap + held-book) × per-symbol cost`, and **the held book is the only term that is neither capped nor cooled down**. Loosening the trigger without touching the cap changes nothing (the cap already binds a fifth of the time). Tightening the trigger without touching the cadence saves little, because 13 ticks × held-book coverage is paid whether or not anything moved. Cutting the cadence is the only lever that scales all three at once — and it is also the only one that directly trades away loss-protection latency, because the same tick carries the free preamble.

### Do not ask the technical seat again when nothing it reads has moved [built 2026-10-01]

**The measurement** [all measured 2026-10-01 against the production DB `/home/qamc/quant-agent/data/quant_agent.db`, read-only, `specialist_evidence` where `agent_name='tech_analyst'`]: 1,763 stock-days, of which **723 were read more than once** by the technical seat and 2,082 of the reads were re-reads. **457 of those 723 (63%) ended the day with the identical rating AND conviction they opened with — 1,056 paid re-reads that returned an answer the desk already held.** The other **266 stock-days DID change verdict intraday**, which is how a position that turns bad gets caught, so re-reading is not waste to be rationed.

**What the verdict is a function of.** The seat is one model call, so its answer is a function of the user message and nothing else. `TechAnalystAgent.build_user_message` builds that message from exactly six per-symbol things: the submitted bars and indicators, the prior-rating context line, the valuation line, the live/forming-session block, and the two run-level macro strings. Those six are the input set.

**Inputs vs outputs on the evidence row**, settled by reading where each field is assigned in `_analyze_chunk`: `rating`, `conviction`, `entry_price`, `reference_target`, `stop_loss`, `support_levels`, `resistance_levels`, `setup_type` and the reasoning fields are **model outputs**; `computed_levels`, `computed_level_touches`, `computed_level_bars`, `levels_coverage`, `signal_bar_low`, `signal_bar_high`, `bars_available` and `atr_14` are **Python-set from the bars** and never asked of the model. The `entry_price` on the row is the seat's *proposed* entry, an output; the entry that is an *input* is the prior position's and arrives via `prior_ratings`.

**Why the recorded fields alone are the WRONG key, measured.** A cache keyed only on those bar-derived fields matches on **1,652 re-reads — and 269 of them had a CHANGED verdict**. They are all derived from completed daily bars, the day's own bar does not close until the market does, and so they cannot see the one input that actually moves intraday: the live price. Keying on them would blind the desk to exactly the changes the re-read exists to catch.

**What shipped.** `tech_input_fingerprint` (`src/research_throttle.py`) hashes all six inputs, exactly — floats by their round-tripping `repr`, so there is no tolerance band, no rounding and no "close enough". `carry_unchanged_tech_reads` reuses a stored verdict only when this run produced a fingerprint, the store holds one, the two are byte-equal, and the stored verdict is readable in full. `TechAnalystAgent.analyze_batch` is now a thin wrapper that drops carried symbols from the batch (and makes **no call at all** when every symbol carries) around the untouched `_analyze_batch_uncached`. `TechStore` persists the fingerprint and the full verdict beside the rating. **No timer, no counter, no cooldown, no sampling rate.** Fails open everywhere: a missing, unreadable or merely ambiguous prior read, or any input that cannot be pinned down exactly, asks the seat.

**A reuse is recorded as CARRIED, never as refreshed.** `TechAnalysisResult.read_state` defaults to `refreshed_this_session` and is set to `carried_forward` — the same `READ_CARRIED` constant item 227's per-seat stamp uses — on a reused verdict, so the freshness record cannot start claiming a read that did not happen.

**What this does NOT yet do, stated plainly.** The saving is **unproven and cannot be proven from the retained record**: the live price at each technical read was never persisted (0 of 3,859 tech evidence rows carry one; `alignment_exit_readings.last_mark_price` joins to none of them). Because the live price is in the key and a traded name's price moves, the hit rate during market hours is expected to be low; the reuse will mostly land on names with no live block at all. The fingerprint now being persisted is what makes the saving measurable from here.

**The stronger "reuse unless the price crossed a level the previous read named" rule was NOT shipped, deliberately.** It is a good idea and the arithmetic is free, but its decisive safety number — how many of the 266 changed verdicts it would have wrongly skipped — is **unmeasurable against the retained record** for the same reason: no price was ever stored beside a tech read, so the replay produces 0 comparable pairs out of 2,082. Shipping it would mean asserting zero wrongly-skipped verdicts on a live book without having measured it, and the claim that the 266 changes must have come from a level crossing is an inference, not a measurement — a verdict can also move on the prior-rating age line, the valuation line, the macro strings, or the model's own non-determinism. Once prices accumulate beside the fingerprints, the replay becomes possible and the crossing rule can be decided on evidence.
