QAMC · how a trade actually gets decided

Trading Desk
Workflow

Five specialist agents read five different kinds of evidence. Each reduces its own read to the same shape — a direction, a strength, a conviction, and a condition that would prove it wrong — before anything is combined, ranked, or acted on.

Price and volume history. Finds real structural support/resistance levels and counts how many times price actually defended each one.

The actual 10‑Q/10‑K filing, pulled from SEC's structured data — not a text scrape. Reduced to a stated call before it reaches the desk; the full filing stays on record for audit, not couriered along by default.

Weight 1.2×

02

Where the verdicts go

deterministic below the AI line
Stage 1 · code, not a model

Ranking

Every seat covering a stock is averaged at its weight — deterministic Python, not an LLM doing arithmetic. This only orders which of several already-eligible stocks comes first; it never decides eligibility and never sizes anything.

Stage 2 · AI

Portfolio Manager

Reads the ranked result plus full account context and decides buy, sell, or hold. It sees the order the desk arrived at — it does not re-weigh the five seats itself.

Stage 3 · AI, independent + verified

Risk Manager

A second, separate AI reviews the proposed trade on its own — a check, not a rubber stamp on the first model's reasoning. A held position stays protected from an early, no-real-reason exit until code confirms the level backing its thesis has actually broken — a closing-price break, held for two trading days, not a single wick and not a calendar countdown. The old rule simply expired every position after a fixed number of days, whether the reason it was bought still held or not.

Data-verified, not calendar‑based · 09‑04
Stage 4 · code, not a model

Deterministic Risk Layer

Real stop-loss floors and position-size caps, computed in Python from real numbers. Nothing above this line can override it.

Final veto
Stage 5

Broker

The order is placed.

Weight vs. conviction

Weight is fixed — set once, per seat, from outside research. It does not change trade to trade. Conviction is not — every seat declares high, medium, or low on every individual call, independent of its weight. A low-weight seat can still call one specific trade with high conviction.

What this doesn't decide

This pipeline decides which stock the desk prefers among several already-eligible ones. How big the trade is still runs on an older, separate mechanism that weighs every seat equally — until the desk has enough of its own resolved trades to earn a different number.
